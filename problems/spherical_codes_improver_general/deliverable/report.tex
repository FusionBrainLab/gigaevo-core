\title{Three Improvements in the Spherical-Code Catalogue,\\
Certified in Exact Arithmetic\\[0.4em]
\large Cases $(d,N) = (24,555)$, $(9,36)$, $(14,154)$}
\author{GigaEvo campaign report}
\date{13 July 2026}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We searched a catalogue of 455 spherical-code cases $(d,N)$ with conjectured-exact
optima and found three whose conjectured optimum is \emph{not} optimal. In each case
the improvement is certified in exact arithmetic from an explicit witness --- no
floating-point quantity enters any accept/reject decision. We prove
\[
\mustar(24,555)\le \tfrac15,\qquad
\mustar(9,36)<\tfrac15,\qquad
\mustar(14,154)<\tfrac{\sqrt{30}}{20},
\]
where $\mustar(d,N)$ denotes the minimum over configurations of the maximum pairwise
inner product. The remaining 452 cases reproduced the catalogue values (every
non-hit gain was below $10^{-15}$). We also report what we could and could not
determine about the true optima, and we are explicit about the limits of each claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Result}

For a configuration $X=\{x_1,\dots,x_N\}\subset S^{d-1}$ write
$\mu(X)=\max_{i<j}\langle x_i,x_j\rangle$, and $\mustar(d,N)=\min_X \mu(X)$.
The catalogue lists, for each case, a conjectured-exact value of $\mustar(d,N)$
together with a construction attaining it. A case is \emph{disproved} when we exhibit
a configuration with $\mu(X)$ strictly below the conjectured value: that refutes the
optimality of the listed construction and the exactness of the listed value.

\section{Certificates}
\label{sec:certs}

\paragraph{$(9,36)$ and $(14,154)$: pointwise rational certificates.}
Each point is an exactly rational point of the sphere. Given an integer vector $a$ and
an integer $D$, we set
\[
\text{num} = (2aD,\; D^2-|a|^2), \qquad \text{den} = D^2+|a|^2,
\]
so that $|\text{num}|^2 = \text{den}^2$ \emph{identically} over $\mathbb{Z}$ --- the
points are exactly on the sphere, with no rounding. Every pairwise inner product is
then compared to the bound in integer arithmetic:
for the bound $1/5$ we check $5\cdot\text{dot} < \text{den}_i\,\text{den}_j$;
for $\sqrt{30}/20$ we check $\text{dot}\le 0$ or
$(20\cdot\text{dot})^2 < 30\,(\text{den}_i \text{den}_j)^2$.
Floating point is used \emph{only} to select the integers $a$; the accept/reject
decision is entirely arbitrary-precision integer arithmetic.

Re-running the standalone verifier on the shipped payloads (this report's artifacts)
gives, in exact rational arithmetic, the following maximum inner products.

\begin{quote}
$(9,36)$, attained by the pair $(19,35)$:
{\small
\[
\mu \;=\; \frac{3289968493528219134480000000}{16461508776597853891737988032}
\;\approx\; 0.199858259542122 \;<\; \tfrac15 .
\]}%
$(14,154)$, attained by the pair $(0,76)$:
{\footnotesize
\[
\mu \;=\; \frac{3999241176665180072971855716360000000000}
               {14610476242607498207941934031493697586588}
\;\approx\; 0.273724217490083 \;<\; \frac{\sqrt{30}}{20}.
\]}
\end{quote}

\paragraph{$(14,154)$: a low-height quadratic is also excluded.}
The natural low-height guess near our first-round numeric --- the larger root of
$17x^2-x-1$, i.e.\ $(1+\sqrt{69})/34$ --- is ruled out \emph{exactly} by the same
certificate (regenerated at $D=10^{10}$): the exact maximum inner product above is
strictly below that root. So the true optimum is not that quadratic irrational.

\paragraph{$(24,555)$: a structural certificate.}
Here the witness is $272$ equiangular lines at $\pm 1/5$ (the regular two-graph on
$276$ vertices minus a $4$-subset containing exactly two coherent triples), together
with $11$ additional points carrying strict slack. The line Gram matrix
$A = I + S/5$ satisfies
\[
A = A[:,J]\; B^{-1} A[J,:] \quad\text{exactly over } \mathbb{Q},
\]
for a $23$-line basis $J$ with $B=A[J,J]\succ 0$ (exact $LDL$, $23/23$ positive pivots).
The identity forces $A$ to be PSD of rank $23$, so the $272$ lines embed exactly in
$\mathbb{R}^{23}$; the $11$ extra points use the remaining dimension and are given by
rational stereographic parameters, with squared norm exactly $1$. The verifier also
re-checks that $S$ is symmetric with zero diagonal and $\pm1$ off-diagonal, i.e.\ that it
really is the Seidel matrix of a set of equiangular lines at angle $\arccos(1/5)$.

All pairwise inner products among the $2\cdot272+11 = 555$ points are then checked over
$\mathbb{Q}$: exact $\max|C_{ij}| = 0.199164088266$ (point/line) and
$\max G_{ij} = 0.199160322560$ (point/point), with zero violations. Coincident points
would surface as an inner product of $1$, so the same checks establish that the $555$
points are distinct. Hence $\mustar(24,555)\le 1/5 < 0.2004565\ldots$

\paragraph{A conjecture in the other direction.}
For $(24,555)$ we also have evidence \emph{for} the construction being beaten by
exactly $1/5$ and no more: the $272$-line core is locally rigid (jitter up to
$3\cdot10^{-2}$ always relaxes back to $1/5$), and unconstrained optimization
plateaus at $1/5 + 8\cdot10^{-6}$. We would conjecture $\mustar(24,555)=1/5$: dropping
from $556$ (where $1/5$ is impossible by the two-graph deep-hole obstruction) to $555$
already releases all the tension.

\section{What the true optima look like}

\paragraph{$(14,154)$: a rigid vertex, and not an algebraic number of catalogue height.}
High-precision refinement lands at a locally rigid KKT vertex,
\[
\mu = 0.2737223554347402446891941044531204632367\ldots
\]
Of the $154$ points, $146$ form a rigid frame with $1807$ contacts, \emph{all} contact
forces strictly positive (minimum multiplier $7.2\cdot10^{-7}$), plus $8$ genuine
rattlers; the nearest non-contact pair sits $3.8\cdot10^{-6}$ below $\mu$. Because the
vertex is nondegenerate, the optimum is an isolated root of a square analytic system,
so Newton is well posed and digits are cheap: mixed-precision iterative refinement
against the frame pins $\mu$ to $1987$ decimal places, two independent runs agreeing
digit for digit.

That precision is what makes an integer-relation search meaningful. Detecting a
degree-$n$ relation of height $H$ requires roughly $(n+1)\log_{10}H$ digits, so we do
not quote one blanket coefficient bound; at each degree we search up to the largest
height the precision actually supports. Working at a $1900$-digit tolerance (comfortably
inside the verified range), PSLQ finds \emph{no} integer relation for any degree
$2 \le n \le 30$, at heights up to $10^{20}$ throughout.

For calibration: the exact values in your catalogue run to degree $14$ with
coefficients below $5\cdot10^{7}$. So the sweep excludes the entire regime of the
table, with roughly twelve orders of magnitude to spare on the height and twice the
degree. The same code recovers $40x^2-3$ for $\sqrt{30}/20$, and recovers the
catalogue's own deepest entry --- the degree-$14$ minimal polynomial at $(3,38)$ ---
exactly, so a null result here is informative rather than a failure to search.

We are not claiming $\mustar(14,154)$ is transcendental. We are claiming it is not an
algebraic number of the size the catalogue's other entries have.

\paragraph{$(9,36)$: a degenerate optimum, and a correction we had to make ourselves.}
This case is harder, and we report a mistake we made and caught. Refinement first
descends to $\mu \approx 0.19985677170944$, which we initially took for the optimum.
It is not: it is a \emph{saddle}. It is a $251$-contact stationary point whose contact
forces cannot be balanced --- the best nonnegative dual leaves residual
$\approx 2\cdot10^{-6}$, so it fails the KKT conditions. Continued descent reaches
\[
\mu \approx 0.199856771597745,
\]
about $1.1\cdot10^{-10}$ below it.

Unlike $(14,154)$, this optimum is \emph{degenerate}: it carries a rattler and flat
contact-preserving directions, so there is no isolated KKT vertex to polish against
and we cannot pin it to comparable precision. Our best value is stable to about $15$
digits across smoothing levels and working precisions. We therefore make \textbf{no
claim} about an exact closed form for $\mustar(9,36)$, and we do not report a PSLQ
verdict for it: at $15$ digits such a sweep could only exclude very low-degree,
tiny-coefficient relations, which would prove nothing.

We note also that an apparent $S_3\times S_3$ symmetry (order $36$) in our first-round
witness is an artefact of an unconverged iterate --- the symmetric configuration is
not locally optimal, and the true optimum is asymmetric.

None of this affects the certificate: $\mustar(9,36)<1/5$ is proved from an exactly
rational witness, and a deeper optimum only widens its margin.

\section{Search protocol and replication}

The search is an evolutionary loop in which a language model proposes and mutates
candidate optimizer \emph{programs}; the artifact that evolves is an optimizer, not a
configuration. A single champion program, held fixed, was then run on all 455 cases
under a fixed protocol (one hour of wall-clock per case per seed).

All 455 cases were run on seeds $0,1,2$; the 404-case subset whose conjectured optimum
is a polynomial root was then re-run on three fresh seeds $3,4,5$. Grading is
best-of-seeds per case, with relative gain banded as
$\text{none} < 10^{-9} \le \text{weak} < 10^{-6} \le \text{moderate} < 10^{-4} \le
\text{strong}$.

\noindent
The three hits are seed-reproducible rather than lucky single-seed basins, and the
three extra seeds surfaced \emph{no} fourth case. One numeric best did improve:
$(24,555)$ went from $0.200008302046$ (seed 2) to $0.200007553381$ (seed 5). This is
irrelevant downstream --- both are above $1/5$, and the structural certificate
$\mustar \le 1/5$ dominates any numeric value --- but we record it because the
identical band counts would otherwise hide it.

Per-case results, including the conjectured value, the best value found, the winning
seed, and the margin, are in \texttt{results.json}.

\section{What we are \emph{not} claiming}

We think it is worth being precise about the scope of these results.

\begin{itemize}
\item We disprove the \textbf{conjectured optimum values} and the optimality of the
listed constructions. We do not claim to refute any published \emph{theorem}.
\item For $(9,36)$, the conjectured value $1/5$ is the value of the Ericson--Zinoviev
(1995) construction. Our result shows that construction is not optimal for $(9,36)$.
We make no assessment of any optimality claim those authors may have made.
\item We do not claim exact values for any of the three true optima. For $(14,154)$ we
show only that it is not algebraic of degree $\le 30$ and height $\le 10^{20}$; that is
an exclusion, not a proof of transcendence. For $(9,36)$ we claim nothing about its
arithmetic nature.
\item The certificates are upper bounds on $\mustar$ from explicit witnesses. They say
nothing about how far below the true optimum might lie, except for $(24,555)$, where
we separately conjecture (but do not prove) $\mustar = 1/5$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Artifacts and how to verify}

Everything needed to check the three claims independently is included. Both verifiers
are stdlib Python 3 --- no numpy, no floating point anywhere in a decision --- they read
the JSON certificates and nothing else, and they exit nonzero on any violation.

\begin{verbatim}
cd certificates

# (9,36):    mu* < 1/5
python3 verify_certificate.py certificate_9_36_rational.json 9 36 "1/5"

# (14,154):  mu* < sqrt(30)/20, and the 17x^2-x-1 root is excluded too
python3 verify_certificate.py certificate_14_154_rational.json 14 154 \
        "sqrt:30/400" "root:17,-1,-1"

# (24,555):  mu* <= 1/5
python3 verify_24_555.py
\end{verbatim}

\noindent
All three commands were re-run from a clean copy of the bundle immediately before this
report was generated, and all pass.
