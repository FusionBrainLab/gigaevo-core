**TASK DEFINITION – HEXAGON PACKING OPTIMIZER**

**Challenge:** Implement a Python class that minimizes the side length of a flat-topped regular enclosing hexagon required to contain $N$ non-overlapping unit regular hexagons.

**Specific Benchmark:** $N=23$.
**General Requirement:** The code must be valid for any $N$ from 1 to 25.

**GEOMETRIC SPECIFICATIONS**
1.  **Unit Hexagons (Items):**
    *   Regular hexagons with circumradius $r = 1.0$.
    *   Side length = 1.0.
    *   May be arbitrarily placed $(x, y)$ and rotated $(\theta)$.
2.  **Enclosing Hexagon (Container):**
    *   Regular, flat-topped, centered at $(0,0)$.
    *   "Flat-topped" vertices are located at angles $[0^\circ, 60^\circ, 120^\circ, 180^\circ, 240^\circ, 300^\circ]$ relative to the center.
    *   Objective: Minimize the enclosing hexagon's side length $L$ (which is equal to its circumradius).

**OBJECTIVE FUNCTION**
Maximize Fitness: $-L$ (Minimize side length $L$).
*Target for N=23:* $L \le 5.39$.

**CONSTRAINTS**
1.  **Containment:** All vertices of every unit hexagon must lie inside or on the boundary of the enclosing hexagon.
2.  **Non-overlap:** The intersection area between any pair of unit hexagons must be zero (touching is allowed).
3.  **Count:** Exactly `hex_num` unit hexagons must be placed.

**EVALUATION BUDGET**
One evaluation gives your class ONE total wall-clock budget. The evaluator calls it repeatedly until that budget is spent, independently validates every configuration it hands back, and keeps the best valid one. Nothing else is rationed, so the cost of a call is a real cost: a cheap call buys more calls, and an expensive call has to earn its keep by being better. Time spent inside one call is time no other call can use.
